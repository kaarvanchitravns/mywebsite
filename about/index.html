About KAARVAN

Moving India’s commercial vehicles.

KAARVAN is building one connected platform for the trucks, fleets and transporters that move the country.

India’s roads are connected. Its fleets mostly are not.

A truck owner pays tolls through one company, tracks his vehicle through another, buys a dashcam from a third, and keeps the account of what each trip earned in a notebook. Four relationships, four support numbers, and no single place where a payment, a location and an expense describe the same vehicle.

KAARVAN exists to close that gap. We build the hardware that goes on the vehicle and the software that makes sense of what it sends back: tolling, tracking, safety and the trip account, on one platform, from one team.

How we build

The hardware is ready to fit and the platform is built and under test. We bring fleets on one at a time, so nothing they depend on breaks while they move to us.

What we publish

Everything we say about the product is something you can see in these pages. Where we quote a figure, it carries a source.

The problem

The problem we are building for

India moves on roughly 12.5 million trucks, and about three and a half million people own them. Three out of four own fewer than five. Only one in ten owns more than ten.

These are not companies with software. The trip is agreed on a phone call, the expense is a receipt in the glovebox, the driver’s balance is a page in a notebook, and the customer’s bill is written by hand. Fewer than two operators in a hundred use any digital freight platform at all.

It is not that they do not want the tools. It is that the tools were built for fleets of two hundred, in English, for someone sitting at a desk. Fuel alone is about half the freight rate, so a trip whose real cost nobody worked out is a trip that may have lost money.

That is what Safar Khata is for: the account behind the trip, on the phone the owner already has, in the language he already speaks.
