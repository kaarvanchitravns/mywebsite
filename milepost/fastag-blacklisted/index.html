FASTag blacklisted: why it happens, and how to clear it

Written for the moment it matters: at the barrier, with a queue behind you.

5 minute read · last checked 20 November 2026

What blacklisted means

A blacklisted tag is one the toll system will not accept. The lane does not open, and you pay in cash — at double the toll, in front of whoever is behind you.1

Inside the NETC system the words have different meanings. Hotlisted is the flag used for security or fraud reasons. Blacklisted is the broader state that covers suspension for any reason at all, insufficient balance included.2 At the plaza you will not be told which one you are.

The real reasons, in order

  • Not enough balance. By a distance the most common cause.3
  • KYC incomplete or expired. The second most common, and the one that catches people out, because the account may be in good standing otherwise.3
  • The tag does not match the vehicle record — a class mismatch, or a tag moved to another vehicle.34
  • Two tags on one vehicle. Duplicates get flagged.4
  • A recharge that has not yet reached the plaza’s system. The money has left your account but NETC has not caught up.4
  • The tag is not stuck to the windscreen. Carrying it loose on the dashboard is a violation, not a workaround.1

Why a tag with money in it still gets refused

This is the complaint we hear most, and the answer is simple: a balance does not protect you. A tag can be blacklisted for KYC, for duplication or for a class mismatch while holding plenty of money.4 Recharging it again does nothing, because money was never the problem.

Timing can also be the culprit. Under NPCI rules in force since 17 February 2025 for tags in exceptional states,3 a tag blacklisted in the hour before it is scanned, or in the ten minutes after, has its transaction declined.5 A recharge made at the barrier can be too late by minutes.

How to clear it

  1. Find out what the status actually is. Check on the NPCI NETC status tool or the My FASTag app, which also tells you which bank issued the tag.4 The NHAI helpline is 1033.6
  2. Fix the cause, not the symptom. Recharge if it was balance. Complete KYC with the issuing bank if it was documentation. Sort out the duplicate or the class if it was either of those.4
  3. Go to the bank that issued the tag, not to NHAI. The issuer holds the account and is the only one who can lift the flag.1
  4. Wait the right amount of time. A low-balance blacklist usually lifts by itself within two to four hours of the recharge. A KYC blacklist takes longer, because a person has to verify the documents.6

How to stop it happening again

Auto-recharge with a threshold set above a single long-route toll removes the leading cause outright.6 For a fleet, the discipline that matters is different: know which tag belongs to which vehicle, and keep the KYC on the account current, because the tag that fails will be the one on the vehicle furthest from home.

Common questions

Why is my FASTag blacklisted even though it has balance?

Because balance is only one of the reasons. Incomplete or expired KYC, a duplicate tag, a vehicle class mismatch or an enforcement flag will blacklist a tag that has plenty of money in it. Recharging again will not help; check the status to find the real cause.

How long does it take to remove a FASTag blacklist?

A low-balance blacklist typically lifts on its own within two to four hours of recharging. A KYC-related blacklist takes longer, because the issuing bank has to verify the documents.

Who do I contact to unblock a FASTag?

The bank that issued the tag, not NHAI. The issuer holds the account and is the only party that can lift the flag. The NHAI helpline 1033 can tell you the status.
